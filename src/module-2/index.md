{% section "Overview", "overview" %}

<p class="note"><strong>Estimated time:</strong> Approximately 3–4 hours</p>

Good data work begins **before** you start analyzing data. Imagine someone at work says, "We need to reduce waste," "Our buses are running late," or "Can you use AI to help us keep more customers?" Before you open a dataset, write code, or ask AI to recommend a solution, you need to understand what problem you are actually trying to solve.

In this module, you will practice turning broad workplace questions into clearly defined data problems. You will identify who will use the results, who may be affected by the work, what decision needs to be supported, what success should look like, what data are needed, and what constraints or risks need to be considered. You will also use AI to help generate questions, identify missing information, compare possible approaches, and challenge assumptions. But AI cannot decide on its own which questions matter most, whether a problem is worth solving, or which tradeoffs are acceptable. Those decisions require your knowledge, judgment, and understanding of the organization and the people involved.

{% section "Essential Question and Objectives", "essential-question-and-objectives" %}

### Essential Question

**What do you need to understand before asking AI to analyze data or recommend a solution?**

### By the End of This Module, You Will Be Able To:

- Translate a real-world workplace question into a clearly defined data problem.
- Identify relevant stakeholders, users, goals, constraints, assumptions, and success criteria.
- Identify the data and contextual information needed to address a problem.
- Identify organizational, user, ethical, and technical considerations that may affect a data solution.
- Use AI to generate clarifying questions and explore possible approaches.
- Critically evaluate AI-generated questions, assumptions, and recommendations.
- Determine where AI can appropriately support your work and where human judgment is required.
