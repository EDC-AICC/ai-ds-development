{% section "Overview" %}

<p class="note"><strong>Estimated time:</strong> Approximately 3–4 hours. <em>Optional examples and challenges can be used for additional practice.</em></p>

Artificial intelligence is changing how data practitioners work. AI can increasingly support tasks such as generating code, preparing data, identifying patterns, creating visualizations, automating processes, and drafting analyses. As AI takes on more of this technical work, your role does not disappear. Instead, more of your attention shifts toward **problem formulation, context, validation, interpretation, and judgment**.

In this module, you will examine how generative AI is changing the day-to-day workflow of a data practitioner. You will compare where practitioners have traditionally spent much of their time with where their effort shifts when AI becomes part of the workflow.

You will also explore why code and analyses that look technically correct can still be wrong for an organizational problem. You will investigate common failure modes of AI-generated data work, including hallucinations, silent logic bugs, inappropriate defaults, and analytical blind spots. Finally, you will practice a human-in-the-loop approach for deciding when an AI-generated result is ready to use, when it needs additional verification or revision, and when it should be rejected.

Throughout this module, keep one central idea in mind:

{% callout "highlight" %}
AI can accelerate data work, but you remain responsible for the quality, context, and consequences of the work.
{% endcallout %}

{% section "What Is an AI-Enhanced Data Practitioner?" %}

The industry-informed profile that serves as the foundation for this course defines the role this way:

> **The AI-Enhanced Data Practitioner, within an organizational context, leverages, orchestrates, assesses, and innovates with AI technology and systems to accelerate and more effectively support the data lifecycle and data governance to collect, transform, and analyze data, build in-house agentic AI tools and fine-tune models, evaluate and communicate results in order to inform and guide decision-making and produce products/services and increase efficiencies.**

You will look at different parts of this definition throughout the course. In this module, you will begin with an especially important part of the role: understanding what AI can accelerate while recognizing what **you** still need to understand, evaluate, verify, and decide.

{% section "Essential Question and Objectives" %}

### Essential Question

**How does AI change your work as a data practitioner, and what knowledge and judgment must you continue to provide?**

### By the End of This Module, You Will Be Able To:

- Describe the role and responsibilities of the AI-Enhanced Data Practitioner.
- Explain how AI is changing common data science tasks and workflows.
- Identify ways AI can support different stages of the data lifecycle.
- Explain why organizational, business, stakeholder, and user context matters in data work.
- Identify tasks and situations in which AI can appropriately support your work.
- Recognize situations requiring significant human expertise, judgment, review, or intervention.
- Explain why AI-generated data, code, analyses, and recommendations must be critically evaluated.
- Identify common risks and failure modes in AI-generated data work.
